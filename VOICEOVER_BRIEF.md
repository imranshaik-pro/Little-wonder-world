# Little Wonder World — Human narrator brief (next audio milestone)

## Why it matters

The current prototype still speaks prompts through the Android device's installed English text-to-speech engine. v1.2 improves pacing and chooses the best local voice when available, but it **does not yet contain a professional human narrator**. Consistent, warm delivery will require a licensed voice-performance pack or properly approved commercial voice-generation service.

## Recording direction

- Audience: ages 2–6. Speak warmly and clearly as though sitting beside one child, not presenting a loud television advertisement.
- Rate: natural and slightly slower than adult conversational speech, without stretching syllables or forcing a high pitch.
- Pacing: one simple idea per sentence; pause briefly after asking a question so the child has time to respond.
- Variation: use curious, encouraging and calm tones. Avoid the same canned praise after every interaction.
- Gain: normalize dialogue to a comfortable consistent level; let animal calls and music dip while the voice speaks.
- Source rights: record original performances; obtain written consent covering offline redistribution in an Android app. Do not imitate identifiable cartoon actors or use commercial nursery-rhyme recordings.

## Short sample script for a voice actor

| Situation | Suggested recording |
| --- | --- |
| Welcome | "Hi, little explorer! Let's discover something wonderful!" |
| Alphabet | "A! A is for apple. Can you say A?" |
| Lowercase | "Here's little a. It makes the same sound!" |
| Counting | "Look! Five stars. Let's count together." |
| Animal clue | "Listen closely. Who makes this sound?" |
| Correct animal | "You found the elephant! Now let's hear its trumpet." |
| Object sound | "What makes that sound? Tap the picture to find out!" |
| Sketchpad | "Choose a color, and draw what you imagine!" |
| Gentle retry | "That's okay! Let's try once more." |
| Praise | "You noticed it! Great listening." |
| Pause | "Your turn. I'm waiting!" |
| Exit | "Lovely exploring. See you next time!" |

## Suggested production order

1. Record 30 universal lines (welcome, directions, praise, retries, exits) in one approved voice.
2. Add 26 letter names and 26 phonics examples; then 100 counted numbers.
3. Add 30 animal names and 40 object names, with pronunciation review.
4. Add separate optional guide-character greetings, preferably by distinct actors or carefully chosen licensed voices.
5. Test in a quiet room with children and parents, adjusting pauses, volume and music ducking based on observed reactions.

Narrator approval and child testing are prerequisites to describing the audio as "natural child-friendly voiceover."
